## How We Work {#how-we-work}

<ol class="principles">
  <li>
    <strong>Progress is measured by research outcomes.</strong>
    <span>Where a project goes is decided by concrete progress toward a publishable paper, not by the number of hours you log.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Students drive their own research.</strong>
    <span>You can pursue your own idea or start from a topic the professor suggests. Either way, you plan and run the experiments, lead the writing, and bring progress to each meeting.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Support grows with momentum.</strong>
    <span>Every student gets a desk and regular guidance from day one. Computing resources, funding, and formal lab affiliation expand as a project shows clear momentum toward a paper. If research progress stops, affiliation, resource access, and confirmation of participation may be paused.</span>
  </li>
</ol>

This keeps things transparent and predictable. If you are looking for a result-driven environment built around publication-quality work, we would love to hear from you.

## What You Will Do {#what-you-will-do}

- **Read foundational NLP papers.** We provide a list of papers related to the lab's research areas. You do not need to read all of them; skim them to build background before you start your own research.
- **Present at the lab seminar.** The lab regularly shares recent research papers. When it is your turn, present a recent paper so that everyone can follow it. The professor may pick the paper, or you can choose one you find interesting.
- **Plan and implement experiments.** You design and run the experiments for a research paper, based on your own idea or on a topic the professor recommends.
- **Weekly 1:1 research meeting.** You meet the professor every week to review progress on the work above and get feedback. If you need to discuss something in between, you can ask for an additional meeting at any time. See the [Research Meeting Guide]({{ '/join/meetings/' | relative_url }}) for how to prepare.

## Lab Life & Probation {#lab-life}

**Your desk.** Every research student is assigned a desk in the student lab.

**Core hours: weekdays 1–4 pm.** The professor regularly drops by the student lab for discussions or calls students to the office, so please be in the lab during these hours. If you have a class at that time, go to class; you do not need to ask. The point is simply to be around during regular working hours so that we can talk when needed. This is not a time sheet; outside these hours, come and go as you like.

**Probation: about two months.** The first two months or so are a probation period, which may vary case by case. During this time we look broadly at your commitment to the lab and whether you can sustain research, in terms of passion and ability.

## Using AI {#using-ai}

We encourage you to use AI to boost your productivity. **Abusing AI is not allowed.** By abuse we mean sharing AI-generated output with other lab members without properly verifying it, or avoiding responsibility for that output. Whatever you share is your work, and you are responsible for it.

## Publication Goals {#publication-goals}

Every student, undergraduate or graduate, aims for a **regular long paper at a top-tier CS conference** such as **ACL, EMNLP, NeurIPS, ICML, or AAAI**, with an **SCIE-indexed journal paper as the minimum**. Demo, workshop, short, and other non-archival papers do not count toward this goal. Other conferences may be considered case by case with prior approval from the professor.

For graduate students, the graduation requirement is two papers in SCIE-indexed Q1–Q2 journals, or one regular long paper at one of the top-tier conferences above.

## After You Join {#after-you-join}

- **When progress stalls.** If progress falls short of expectations for a sustained period, the professor may offer additional guidance, adjust the research scope, or discuss your research direction and continuation in the program.
- **Funded projects and compensation.** Participation in funded projects is open to students with clear, paper-oriented progress. Compensation is decided month by month based on verified progress toward a publishable paper, and may be paused or discontinued during periods without tangible research output, at the professor's discretion.
