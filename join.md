## How We Work {#how-we-work}

<ol class="principles">
  <li>
    <strong>Progress is measured by research outcomes.</strong>
    <span>We look at concrete progress toward a publishable paper, not effort, attendance, or hours spent in the lab.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Students lead their own research.</strong>
    <span>You define the problem, run the experiments, lead the writing, and initiate and prepare research meetings. Meetings are for reviewing and refining research, not for reminders or task management.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Support grows with momentum.</strong>
    <span>Computing resources, funding, and official lab affiliation start small and expand once a project shows clear momentum toward a paper. Affiliation reflects active participation: if research progress stops, affiliation, resource access, and confirmation of participation may be paused.</span>
  </li>
</ol>

This keeps things transparent and predictable. Students who consistently produce results get focused guidance, more resources, and formal affiliation. If you are looking for a result-driven environment built around publication-quality work, we would love to hear from you.

## Who Can Apply {#who-can-apply}

**Timing.** We accept undergraduate research students in their third year or above. Starting before your final year gives you time to carry a project through to publication before you graduate. Students who plan to apply to graduate programs and want intensive research experience are especially encouraged to apply.

**Coding skills.** You should be comfortable with basic data structures and algorithms. As a reference, you should be able to solve [this list of LeetCode problems](https://leetcode.com/problem-list/vur1tcd5/) within a day of focused effort. In our experience, most upper-year students in the KNU School of Computer Science can solve them without much difficulty, and we expect every lab member to be able to, whatever their research focus.

**English.** Most papers and technical materials are in English, so you should be able to read academic papers without substantial help. As a practical reference, current members have a TEPS score of 268 or higher or a TOEIC score of 780 or higher. These are indicators, not strict cutoffs.

## Publication Goals {#publication-goals}

Every student works toward independent, sustained research that leads to high-impact publications.

| Program | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Undergraduate research | At least one paper in a KCI-indexed journal |
| Graduate (to graduate) | Two papers in SCIE-indexed Q1–Q2 journals, **or** one regular long paper at a top-tier international conference |
{: .goal-table }

For conferences, only **regular long papers** count; demo, workshop, short, and other non-archival papers do not. Valid venues are **ACL, EMNLP, NeurIPS, ICML, and AAAI**. Other conferences may be considered case by case with prior approval from the supervising professor.

## After You Join {#after-you-join}

- **Monthly meetings.** Progress is reviewed in a monthly meeting with the supervising professor. You are responsible for scheduling and preparing it; regular, self-directed communication is a core part of the program, and if meetings lapse, participation may end. See the [Monthly Meeting Guide]({{ '/join/meetings/' | relative_url }}) for how to prepare.
- **Supporting tasks.** Alongside your main project, you may be asked to help with literature reviews, implementations, or reproductions of existing methods. These build the skills you need for independent research.
- **When progress stalls.** If progress falls short of expectations for a sustained period, the professor may offer additional guidance, adjust the research scope, or discuss your research direction and continuation in the program.
- **Funded projects and compensation.** Participation in funded projects is open to students with clear, paper-oriented progress. Compensation is decided month by month based on verified progress toward a publishable paper, and may be paused or discontinued during periods without tangible research output, at the professor's discretion.
